How Supervision decides 🧠

The one job: no sustained crash cycles, with the widest life that goal allows. Everything below is an experiment with a review date, not a diagnosis. The number that judges any day is the morning two days later.

What it looks at

The day, one block at a time

When a break ends, one tap ("how did that go?") releases the screen, and a small card pops up top-right: overall, how's my head, clear (no brain fog), energy and not crashing, each 1 = really bad, 10 = the best, plus a one-line note for what you did ("drank water"). All optional, never blocking. The same card opens any time you rest the pointer in the top-right corner. Only clear, head and not-crashing count (energy and overall are logged, never gated). "Down" is compared with your first answer to that question today; the second hour-off in a day ends the day (after a break or from the top-right corner check), so a foggy morning doesn't count against you. Stopping at the gate is the experiment working, not a failure.

Blocks follow how you are

Green · recovery ≥ 67

First block 30 min, 5-min breaks. Walks, Pilates (once, before 3 pm), foot PT, stretching, journal, plan, rap, poetry.

Yellow · 34–66

First block 25 min, 7-min breaks. Stretch, foot PT, journal, plan, music.

Red · < 34

First block 15 min, 10-min breaks. Rest and music only.

Short sleep, a travel day or a stress flag start you 5 min shorter. After that, every how-are-you card moves the next block: steady or better with overall 7+ → +5 min; clear, head or not-crashing down 1 → −5 min; always between 15 and 45. Skip the card and it stays put. No Whoop reading yet? Treated as yellow.

Breaks are for standing up, stretching, looking far away (20-20-20) and planning what's next. Every 2 hours one of them is a 15-minute lie-down.

One cap: 8 hours on the computer

Every active minute at the screen counts, meetings and scrolling included. At 8 hours the screen locks for the day: whatever's left can happen off the computer. The kinds of work are still weighted in the logs (meetings 1.3×, intense meetings 1.6×, admin 0.8×…) so we can learn which work costs you most, but they don't lock anything.

Nights

From 10 pm the computer blinks: 2 minutes of work, then a 10-minute break, with no nudges or cards in between. At midnight it's off until 6 am, however much of the 8 hours is left.

Earning it back

Clean means: neither following morning is red, resting HR within +4 of your 28-day median, no crash flagged, the gate didn't end the day. Starting blocks shrink by 5 min for 14 days only on a pattern: a crash, two red mornings in a week, or resting HR up 5+ for three days straight. A single bad day changes that day only.

Breaks: what you get and why

Every break screen shows the activity, the instructions, the numbers behind the choice, and up to three alternatives you can tap. When it keeps you on plain rest it says what you'd rather be doing, the specific gate ("you're at 140 of 150"), and what would unlock the other options. Movement is always followed by rest; never two active breaks in a row; none after 5 pm.

What never happens

  • Missing a meeting: a break is trimmed to end 5 minutes before your next meeting (video link or other attendees), and any longer lock steps aside 5 minutes before and comes back after if there's time left.
  • A lock during a call: calendar events with video links (±10 min), Zoom's in-meeting process, camera and meeting windows all defer it.
  • More than one nudge per block. The how-are-you card pops up after a break but never blocks anything; eat/water reminders ride on the break screen.
  • The words "push through" or "dangerous." A missed limit is logged as data.
  • A capacity change from one bad day.

Talking to it

The only way to pause or adjust is asking Claude, who logs the reason. Useful things to say: "pause for an hour, I'm in a crunch" · "that meeting was intense" · "I did 30 minutes on paper" · "flag travel" · "what did I get done today?" · "how's the experiment going?"
