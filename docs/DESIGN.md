# Design: why Supervision works the way it does

Supervision is built for people whose cognitive effort has a **delayed** cost: ME/CFS,
Long COVID with post-exertional malaise (PEM), and similar conditions. The bill for a hard
afternoon often arrives 12–48 hours later, so "stop when it hurts" is always too late.

## The goal
**No sustained crash cycles, with the widest life that goal allows.** Not maximum
productivity, not maximum restriction.

## Two hypotheses, held open
Clinicians disagree. One view (much of the patient-organization and CDC guidance): exertion
beyond your envelope triggers PEM, repeated PEM is associated with worse long-term function,
so pace pre-emptively. Another view (some neurologists and rehab clinicians): carefully
managed activity doesn't cause lasting damage, and fear-driven withdrawal and deconditioning
are the bigger risk; do what you could repeat tomorrow, and if your response is ~48 h
delayed, alternate heavy and light days.

Supervision doesn't pick a side. It starts conservative, measures your delayed response, and
lets the data move the numbers. Every restriction is shown as an experiment with a review
date.

## The rules
1. **"Repeatable tomorrow."** The daily budget is a guess at the load you could do again
   tomorrow. The verdict on any day is the two mornings after it.
2. **Tiered blocks.** The morning recovery reading sets block length (30 / 25 / 15 min) and
   the day's base budget (180 / 150 / 90 weighted minutes). No reading = the middle tier.
3. **Weighted, not raw.** Kinds of work cost different amounts. Video meetings and
   emotionally intense meetings cost the most; admin and email the least; heavy
   context-switching adds a penalty. Off-screen work (paper, voice) counts at half.
   The weights are starting guesses, reviewed every two weeks against your data.
4. **The 3-day cap is the real guard rail.** One big day is fine. Three in a row isn't:
   the rolling 3-day load can't exceed 2.5× your base.
5. **Symptom gate.** After every break, three faces (brain fog, headache/neck, how crashy).
   Compared with the day's first reading: up 1 → gentler blocks, rest-only breaks; up 2
   (or any face at 4+) → done for today.
6. **Earn it back.** Three clean days (judged at day+2) → +20 minutes base, +5 minute blocks.
   Capacity drops only on a pattern (a crash, two red mornings in a week, resting HR up 5+
   for three days), for 14 days, and is earned back the same way. One bad day changes that
   day only.
7. **Rest is pre-emptive.** Scheduled, not symptom-triggered. Real rest is lying down, eyes
   closed; music is fine; screens and podcasts are not rest. A long horizontal rest every 2 h.
8. **Movement is its own track.** Gated by the morning tier and a heart-rate ceiling
   (resting HR + 15), not by the work budget, and always followed by rest.
9. **Alert fatigue is the enemy.** One nudge per block, one lock, at most two check-in cards
   a day. Care reminders (eat, water) ride on the break screen.
10. **Every decision explains itself**, with numbers, and names what you'd rather be doing.
11. **The only override is a conversation.** Pausing happens through Claude, with the reason
    logged, so a tired afternoon brain can't quietly undo what a clear morning brain decided.
